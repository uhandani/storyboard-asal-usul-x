# Storyboard: "The Thing" (The Origin of x)

Length: 30 s · 16:9 · no text (the equation is shown with pebbles on a balance scale, never with numbers)

**Logline:** Clawd, a scholar in Baghdad, wants to name a "thing" whose value is unknown, but the name is hard to pass on across languages and ages, so it ends up as two crossed brush strokes: **x**.

**Story basis (in brief):**
- 9th century, Baghdad: al-Khwarizmi's algebra calls the unknown *al-shay'*, "the thing".
- Spain/Toledo: according to a popular story, translators had no letter for the "sh" sound, so they used the Greek χ (chi), which became Latin *x*.
- 1637: Descartes, in *La Géométrie*, uses x, y, z for unknowns. This is the documented history, and it is what made x the standard.
- (The "shay → chi → x" story is popular but disputed among historians. The film shows it as a passed-on legend, then closes with the printing press, which is the documented part.)

**World:** one thread of light across eras, carried by the same object: a **cloth-wrapped bundle** (the "thing").
- A, Baghdad: indigo night, a warm amber oil lamp.
- B, Toledo: dusty ochre stone, a slanting afternoon window.
- C, printing house: sepia, ink-black, paper cream.
- D, classroom today: dusty green chalkboard, warm morning light.
- Colour arc: night → afternoon → morning. The mystery lightens as the "thing" gets its name.

**Motif:** the **balance scale** and the **bundle**. In A, the bundle on the scale can't be opened (unknown). In D, the same scale appears on the chalkboard, the bundle is opened, and the answer glows.

**Clawd's arc:** curious → frustrated (stuck on the sound) → idea → busy/happy (the mark spreads) → amazed → proud.
